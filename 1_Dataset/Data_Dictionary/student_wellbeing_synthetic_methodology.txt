STUDENT WELLBEING SYNTHETIC DATASET
===================================

File: student_wellbeing_synthetic_2000.csv
Rows: 2,000 fully synthetic records
Audience represented: illustrative ages 14–16, Classes 9–10
Questions: Q01–Q20, matching the proposed first-person questionnaire.

IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS
---------------------
- Every row is artificially generated. No real student data is included.
- The data must not be described as collected from students, representative of students, or clinically validated.
- The response patterns are simulated for software development and capstone demonstration only.
- Gender is generated as a demographic field and is not used to determine answers, scores, or labels.
- The illustrative wellbeing index and bands are transparent rule-based constructs, not validated measures.
- Do not present these bands as diagnoses, mental-health screening outcomes, or reliable assessments of real students.
- A model trained to predict the illustrative band would mainly learn the scoring rules used to create that band. Its accuracy would not demonstrate real-world predictive validity.

SCORING DESIGN
--------------
1. Each response is encoded from 1 (Strongly disagree) to 5 (Strongly agree).
2. Negative/challenge statements are reverse-coded: 1 becomes 5, 2 becomes 4, 3 stays 3, 4 becomes 2, 5 becomes 1.
3. Within each of the seven dimensions, the item scores are averaged.
4. The overall illustrative index is the equal-weight average of the seven dimension scores.
5. Provisional demonstration bands:
   - Below 2.5: More support may be helpful
   - 2.5 to below 3.7: Some areas may need a check-in
   - 3.7 or above: Generally positive indicators

These thresholds are arbitrary demonstration thresholds. They require review and validation before any use with real students.

MODEL DEVELOPMENT RECOMMENDATION
---------------------------------
For the next capstone stage, use this dataset to validate the data pipeline, EDA, visualisations, missing-value handling, and app integration. Clearly label any classification exercise as learning to reproduce an illustrative rule-based band. Do not claim it predicts actual wellbeing. For a defensible real-world model, obtain appropriate expert guidance and ethically collected, consented, validated outcome data.
